Investigating an “Access Denied” Issue

01

Scenario

A user can reach an internal application but receives an “Access Denied” message after signing in. The investigation must distinguish an authentication problem from an authorization or entitlement issue.

Simulated caseIAMAccess control
02

Investigation checklist

  • Confirm the user identity and exact error/time.
  • Check whether the account is active and authentication succeeds.
  • Review assigned roles, groups, and application entitlements.
  • Compare expected access with the approved request or policy.
  • Review relevant logs, where authorized and available.
03

Possible causes to validate

  • Required role or group is missing.
  • Access request has not been approved or fully provisioned.
  • Group membership or entitlement has not synchronized.
  • Account status or policy conditions block access.
  • Application-side permissions differ from identity-platform assignments.
04

Resolution and verification

  • Follow the organization’s approval and least-privilege process.
  • Correct only the verified assignment or configuration issue.
  • Ask the user to retest and confirm the required function works.
  • Record the cause, action, evidence, and outcome in the ticket.
  • Escalate if logs or permissions are managed by another team.
Additional troubleshooting areas

IAM & Security troubleshooting scenarios

These are generalized troubleshooting patterns for common IAM/security issues. Each scenario is illustrative and must be adapted to the actual tools, procedures, and evidence available in a given environment.

05

Login & authentication failures

Symptom: A user cannot sign in or repeatedly receives an authentication error.

  • Confirm the exact error, time, application, and scope of impact.
  • Check account status and whether the issue affects one or multiple users.
  • Review available authentication events and relevant policy conditions.
  • Check time synchronization or identity-provider dependencies where applicable.
  • Retest after an approved correction and document the outcome.

06

Access denied & permission mismatch

Symptom: Sign-in succeeds, but a user cannot open an application or perform an expected action.

  • Separate successful authentication from failed authorization.
  • Compare required access with approved roles, groups, and entitlements.
  • Check whether changes have propagated to the target application.
  • Apply only approved, least-privilege changes.
  • Confirm the intended function works after remediation.

07

Provisioning & deprovisioning

Symptom: A new user does not receive expected access, or access remains after a role or employment change.

  • Verify the approved request and source-of-truth user attributes.
  • Check the provisioning status and available connector or workflow errors.
  • Compare the intended entitlement with the target-system account state.
  • For removal, validate completion and follow the defined offboarding controls.
  • Record exceptions and escalate failed or delayed workflows.

08

Account lockout & account status

Symptom: A user is locked out or an account appears disabled or inactive.

  • Verify the account state and relevant event timestamps.
  • Look for repeated failed sign-ins or stale saved credentials where evidence permits.
  • Check relevant lockout policy and dependent services.
  • Unlock or restore an account only under approved identity-verification procedures.
  • Monitor for recurrence and document the likely cause.

09

PAM & privileged access

Symptom: An authorized administrator cannot retrieve a privileged credential or start an approved session.

  • Verify the requester, approval, target, and permitted access window.
  • Check safe/vault access, account onboarding state, and relevant session policy where applicable.
  • Review available audit events and connectivity prerequisites.
  • Do not expose passwords, secrets, session recordings, or privileged data in public notes.
  • Escalate platform-side issues with sanitized evidence and verify access safely.

10

AD/LDAP, SSO & MFA integration

Symptom: Authentication or group-based access behaves differently between an identity source and an application.

  • Identify the affected authentication flow and the systems involved.
  • Validate directory reachability and relevant user/group attributes when authorized.
  • Check SSO configuration and error details without publishing tokens or assertions.
  • For MFA, verify enrollment and policy requirements using approved support procedures.
  • Correlate available logs across systems and escalate with timestamps and sanitized errors.
